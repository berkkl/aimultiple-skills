# Research depth — detail

## Topic understanding

No sentence may be written that the author cannot defend unaided. The test: could the author explain this concept at a whiteboard for five minutes without notes? If not, keep reading before writing.

Machine work (paraphrasing, summarizing known material) is not analyst work. The article has to add something.

## Required reading before drafting

1. Top 3 US Google results for the target keyword. Note their angles, structure, sources.
2. Previous AIMultiple articles on adjacent topics. Reference them if relevant.
3. Recent (<2 years) reports from: McKinsey, BCG, Bain, IBM, Google, Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC, Gartner, Forrester, IDC, G2.
4. AIMultiple great reports folder on Google Drive.
5. Surfer SERP Analyzer for the keyword. Open competitors with content score >66 first. Take notes on: titles, H2s, H3s, images, tables, video count.

## Actionable recommendations

The article must deliver specific, situation-aware recommendations. Generic statements ("companies should consider X") are slop. Good recommendations name the situation and the trade-off:

> An SMB with limited engineering resources should adopt a pre-trained model. An enterprise needing a strategic capability should train its own model on proprietary data.

If the article cannot produce at least one recommendation of this quality, kill it.

## Subtopic discovery

Use all three:
- Surfer SERP Analyzer → Questions (search volume + overlap count per question)
- answerthepublic.com
- alsoasked.com

Pick subtopics with actual search demand, not just ones that sound interesting.

## Archetype selection

Every article maps to an archetype (vendor list, vs article, use-case article, how-to, etc.). Pick one and follow its structural template. Tag the article with the archetype on publish.

## Sedat card gate

Vendor-feature articles (any article that will feature or compare products) require a Sedat-reviewed card before writing. Vedat opens the card. Do not start drafting until comments come back.

## Kill criteria

Do not write the article if:
- Only one credible source exists on the topic.
- The author cannot articulate one original insight the top 3 Google results miss.
- The topic is not a fit for AIMultiple (consumer, non-B2B, non-tech, or no vendor comparison angle).
