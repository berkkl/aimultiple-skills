# Reader review of a benchmark article before publication

Read the draft at the path given at the end of this file. Review it as the reader it is written for: someone technically literate who does not work in this subfield and is making a genuine effort to follow it. You are not the author's editor and you are not here to improve the prose.

Answer three questions, in this order, with line or sentence quotes for every finding.

## 1. Does every sentence parse?

Flag any sentence that:
- has no subject or no finite main verb (an appositive plus a relative clause is a fragment)
- puts so much before the subject that the reader loses the thread
- has a pronoun or a "these"/"those"/"it" whose antecedent is not the nearest plausible noun
- needs a second read for a reason other than the difficulty of the idea

Quote the sentence. Say what is missing. Do not rewrite it.

## 2. Does the headline claim match the statistics in the body?

Take the strongest claim in the title, the intro and the first takeaway under each chart. For each one, find what the body actually establishes and compare:
- Is the claim asserted for cases the body says were never tested?
- Does the body label a number descriptive and the headline treat it as a result?
- Does a correction for multiple comparisons apply, and does the headline respect what survived it?
- Is a confidence interval, rank range or clique reported in the body but dropped from the claim?

Name every claim that outruns its evidence and say by how much.

## 3. Is anything undefined or contradicted?

- A term of art used before it is defined, or never defined.
- A characterization of a named method, model or dataset that contradicts that thing's own source. Check the source's own title and abstract where you can.
- A number that appears with different precision or a different value in two places.
- More than three decimal places on any figure outside a p-value in scientific notation.

## Rules

- Quote, then diagnose. No rewrites, no suggested replacement sentences, no style opinions.
- Do not propose adding words for flow, transitions or context. The draft was deliberately cut and restoring padding is the failure mode of this review.
- Rank findings by whether a reader would be misled, then by whether a reader would be confused. Cosmetic issues go last or not at all.
- If a section is clear and correctly hedged, say so and move on.
